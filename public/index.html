A floor company that starts with the slab

Prescott Valley has grown fast, and a lot of its homes are relatively new. That is good news for floor coatings: newer slabs are often in great shape. But it also means many garages still have the builder's sealer on them, and many owners have already tried a quick coating that did not last through the first winter.

We do this work differently. Every project starts with an honest look at the concrete. We check for curing compounds, sealers, moisture, cracks and winter damage, then grind and repair the slab before any coating goes down. Only then do we choose a system, and we choose it for how you actually use the space.

That might be a garage floor coating with a decorative flake finish, a seamless residential floor for a basement, laundry room or casita, a metallic epoxy feature floor, a fast-curing polyaspartic system, a tough polyurea base for a workshop, a textured patio coating, or a durable floor for a business or warehouse. Estimates are free, done in person and always in writing.

New build or older home: what changes

The approach depends on the age and history of the slab.

Homes built in the last few years

The slab is usually in good shape, but builder sealers and a weak, powdery top layer must be ground off. We also confirm the concrete has cured long enough before coating.

Homes ten to twenty years old

Expect some winter flaking near the door, oil stains and hairline cracks. Grinding and repairs take a little longer, and a flexible base may be worth it.

Older homes and previous coatings

Paint, failed kits and old sealers all come off first. Cracks and spalls are repaired so the new floor starts on a sound surface.

Garage epoxy flooring that survives a valley winter

In Prescott Valley, the garage floor takes the hardest hits. Snow and slush ride in on wheels a few times each winter, bringing deicer and grit with them. Summer brings hot tires and strong afternoon sun through the door. Spring brings wind and dust. Bare concrete slowly stains, pits and flakes under all of it.

Our garage systems start with diamond grinding to remove the builder's sealer and open the concrete, followed by crack repair. Then comes a bonding base coat, a decorative flake broadcast and a UV-stable, slip-resistant topcoat.

  • Removes builder sealers that block adhesion
  • Repairs winter flaking and pitting before coating
  • Texture for safe footing when snowmelt comes in

Local climate and what it means for your floor

At about five thousand feet, Prescott Valley gets a little of everything. Summers are warm with strong sun, monsoon storms bring heavy rain and dust, and winters bring freezing nights and the occasional snowfall. Spring can be windy and dry. Each season leaves its mark on concrete.

  • WinterFreeze-thaw and deicer cause flaking near the garage door. Sealed floors keep moisture out.
  • SpringWind blows fine dust into every garage. Flake finishes hide it between cleanings.
  • SummerStrong UV yellows standard epoxy. We use UV-stable topcoats.
  • MonsoonStorm water and mud come in fast. Textured topcoats keep floors safe.

My home is only a few years old. Does the garage still need grinding?

Yes. Builders often apply a curing compound or sealer to new garage slabs, and the surface layer of fresh concrete is weak and powdery. Both stop a coating from bonding properly. Diamond grinding removes them and opens the concrete so the coating can grip.

What causes the white haze and flaking near my garage door?

Usually winter. Snowmelt and deicer from the roads drip off vehicles, soak into the concrete and freeze overnight. Over a few seasons, that breaks down the surface. A sealed, coated floor keeps water and salt out of the slab.

Can I still park in the garage during the project?

Not while we work, but most garages are back in use quickly. With a fast-curing topcoat, many are ready for foot traffic the same day and vehicles within a day or two, depending on temperature.

What does a garage floor coating cost in Prescott Valley?

Every garage prices differently. A recent build with a clean slab is a simpler job than an older three-car garage with paint to strip and winter damage to fix. We come out, measure and put two or three options in writing so you can choose.

What is the best time of year for a floor project here?

Spring and fall are ideal: mild days, cool nights and dry air. Summer works well with early starts. Winter is fine for heated garages and interiors; unheated spaces may need temporary heat or a cold-tolerant system.

Do you charge for estimates?

No. Estimates are free, done in person and given in writing, with no obligation.

How far out are you booking?

It varies by season. The best way to find out is to request an estimate. When we follow up, we will give you a realistic install window.

How do I clean a coated floor?

Sweep or dust mop regularly, and mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner when needed. Avoid citrus degreasers and harsh solvents. In winter, squeegee meltwater out of the garage and rinse off deicer residue now and then.

Will it be slippery?

We add slip-resistant texture to garage and entry floors as standard. Flake floors also have natural grip. Interior floors can be given a fine texture where needed.

Does my HOA need to approve it?

Interior garage floors usually do not need approval, but anything visible from the street, such as driveways or front walks, often does. Check your community guidelines. We can provide product information for any submission.
