Decorative flake floors with a valley palette

A flake floor looks like natural stone from standing height and hides the dust, tire marks and scuffs that make plain floors look tired. It is the most popular finish we install, for good reason.

Ask most people what a "finished garage floor" looks like and they will describe a flake floor: a speckled, granite-like surface with a clear, glossy topcoat. It has become the standard for good reason. The chips add texture and visual noise that hide everyday dirt, and the layered system underneath is tough enough for vehicles, tools and years of use.

What many homeowners do not realize is how much control they have over the look. The size of the chips, the color blend and how densely the chips are spread can make the same system look bold and rugged or quiet and refined. This page explains those choices so you can pick a floor that suits your home.

What goes into a flake floor

A flake floor is built in layers. After the slab is diamond-ground and repaired, a pigmented base coat is rolled out. While it is still wet, colored vinyl flakes are thrown across it by hand, settling into the resin. Once the base cures, the excess flakes are swept up and the surface is scraped smooth.

The final layer is a clear topcoat. It locks the flakes in, sets the sheen and provides the chemical, abrasion and UV resistance. It is also where texture is added for slip resistance.

The flakes themselves are decorative, but they do practical work: they hide imperfections in the slab, add a little thickness and break up the surface so dust and marks are far less noticeable.

Blends that suit Prescott Valley homes

There are dozens of stock blends to choose from. These are the families that look most at home in the valley.

Sage meadow

Soft green-grey, cream and tan. Calm and natural, echoing the grassland around town.

Granite stone

Grey, white and black. A clean, versatile look that works with almost any exterior.

High desert

Tan, rust and brown. Warm and earthy, and excellent at hiding dust and dried mud.

Mingus slate

Charcoal, grey and a touch of blue. Bold and modern, great for hiding tire marks.

The base coat is tinted to match the dominant tone in your blend, so the floor looks consistent even where the chip coverage is a bit lighter. We bring physical samples to every estimate so you can compare them on your own floor.

Where flake floors are used

Garages are the obvious home for flake floors. See our garage coatings page for how we build them. But flake systems also work beautifully in laundry rooms and mudrooms, where a fine blend hides dust and handles water; in workshops and hobby spaces, where dropped tools and spills are part of the routine; on covered patios with an exterior topcoat; and in businesses such as retail stores, auto showrooms and gyms that need a durable, attractive floor that looks clean between cleanings.

Our flake installation process

  1. Grind and repair

    Builder sealers, coatings and weak concrete are ground off; cracks and pits are filled.

  2. Tinted base coat

    Applied in sections sized for the day’s temperature.

  3. Hand broadcast

    Flakes are thrown in overlapping passes for even coverage.

  4. Scrape and vacuum

    Loose and raised chips are removed after the base cures.

  5. Clear topcoat

    A UV-stable, textured topcoat completes the system.

Flake floors through the valley seasons

Prescott Valley's seasons each test a floor. Spring wind blows fine dust into garages. Monsoon storms bring mud and water. Winter brings slush and deicer residue. A mid-tone flake blend hides all of it between cleanings, which is the main reason it is so popular here.

Temperature affects installation. On cool mornings, base coats thicken and take longer to accept flakes evenly; in summer heat, they set faster. We adjust timing, batch size and sometimes product choice to match the conditions.

And because sunlight at this elevation is strong, we use UV-stable topcoats on any floor that gets direct light through a door or window.

The details that make a flake floor look right

You can spot a rushed flake floor from the driveway: thin patches where the chips ran out, heavy clusters where a handful landed at once, and ragged lines along the door threshold. Underfoot, a skimpy clear coat leaves it scratchy and hard to mop. Getting an even spread, a smooth top and crisp edges is a matter of practice, and it is the part of the job we are proudest of.

Caring for a flake floor

A push broom or dust mop handles most weeks. Once in a while, give it a wash with a mild, neutral cleaner and a soft-bristle brush. Through winter, hose or mop away the white film deicer leaves behind. Skip orange-oil degreasers and harsh solvents. Years down the line, the clear layer can be lightly sanded and renewed while the chips stay untouched underneath.

What affects the price

Price starts with the size of the floor, then moves with how much work the concrete needs. A new-build slab with builder sealer is quick to grind; a flaking older slab takes longer. Full-coverage chips cost more than a light scatter, and a made-to-order blend more than a stock one. Every one of those items appears as its own line on your estimate.

Flake floors in HOA communities

Interior garage floors rarely need HOA approval, but many Prescott Valley homeowners still like a finish that looks tidy and neutral when the door is up. Mid-tone stone, granite and sage blends fit naturally with the earth-tone exteriors common in master-planned neighborhoods. They also hide the spring dust that blows under every door in the valley. If you are coating an exterior area as well, such as a front entry, we can suggest coordinating colors that are easy to get approved.

Small spaces, big difference

Flake is not only for garages. In laundry rooms, mudrooms, pantries and utility closets, a finer chip in a light blend makes a small room feel cleaner and brighter, and it handles water from washers and wet boots without fuss. These rooms are quick to coat and often take less than a day of active work, making them an easy upgrade to add when we are already coating the garage.

Flake floor questions

Straight answers. If yours is missing, call (928) 555-0139.

Which blend hides dust best?

Mid-tone blends with tan, grey and some darker chips hide dust and dried mud best. Very light or very dark blends show more.

Is a flake floor rough to walk on?

With a proper topcoat it has a fine, even texture, comfortable underfoot and easy to mop.

Can I choose my own color mix?

Yes, a made-to-order mix can be ordered if none of the stock options feel right. Expect a longer wait for the material, so plan a few extra weeks.

Do the flakes fade?

Under a good clear coat, chip colors stay put for years. Where afternoon sun reaches the floor, a sun-resistant top layer keeps both the chips and the clear coat from shifting color.

How long does it take to install?

Plan on a day or two of work for most garages, then a short wait before parking. Cooler months add a little time.

Can a flake floor go in a laundry room?

Yes. Smaller chips in a light blend work beautifully and handle water and detergent with ease.

Will the chips come loose?

No. They are fully sealed under the topcoat and do not wear off with normal use.

Can you coat the garage and laundry room at the same time?

Yes, and many homeowners do. Coordinating blends, often a standard chip in the garage and a finer version in the laundry room, gives a consistent look.
